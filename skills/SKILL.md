# Nature Scientific Writing Guidelines

## Core Principles

Effective writing must be **clear, accurate, and concise**. The goal is to enable readers to understand your ideas effortlessly, unambiguously, and rapidly.

### Basic Guidelines

- ✅ Write in a straightforward way
- ✅ No need to write in unusual, complicated, or overly formal ways to "sound scientific"
- ✅ If you can tell a friend about your work, you're off to a good start

---

## ⚠️ CRITICAL RULES FOR AI ASSISTANTS

### Rule 1: Preserve Original Logic

**DO NOT modify the original logic or meaning of the text. Only optimize formatting, layout, and readability.**

- ✅ ALLOWED: Formatting improvements, layout optimization, readability enhancements
- ❌ FORBIDDEN: Changing the logical flow, modifying arguments, altering conclusions, rewriting content

### Rule 2: User Approval Required

**WAIT for user approval before making EACH modification.**

**Workflow:**
1. Analyze the text and identify formatting/layout issues
2. Present proposed changes to the user
3. **WAIT** for explicit user approval
4. Only apply approved changes
5. Repeat for each section/modification

## I. Sentence Construction

### ⚠️ 1.0 CRITICAL: Every Sentence Must Have Purpose

**Every sentence you write must serve a clear purpose.** This is the foundational rule of effective writing.

#### Sentence Purpose Test

Before writing any sentence, ask yourself:

| Question | If No → |
|----------|---------|
| Does this sentence convey information? | Delete it |
| Does this sentence advance the argument? | Delete it |
| Does this sentence help the reader understand? | Delete it |
| Can the reader understand the text without this sentence? | Delete it |

#### Types of Valid Sentence Purposes

Every sentence must fulfill at least ONE of these purposes:

1. **Convey new information**
   - ✅ "The enzyme activity increased by 45% under optimal conditions."
   - ❌ "It is important to note that..." (vague, no actual information)

2. **Establish context or background**
   - ✅ "Previous studies established the role of temperature in enzyme kinetics."
   - ❌ "As we all know, science is important." (obvious, no value)

3. **Create logical transition**
   - ✅ "These findings suggest a potential mechanism for the observed phenomenon."
   - ❌ "Moving on to the next point..." (lazy transition, no substance)

4. **Support or evidence a claim**
   - ✅ "The p-value of 0.001 confirms statistical significance."
   - ❌ "This is clearly important." (unsupported assertion)

5. **Define or clarify**
   - ✅ "Photosynthesis converts light energy into chemical energy stored in glucose."
   - ❌ "Photosynthesis is an interesting process." (vague, uninformative)

#### ❌ Types of Meaningless Sentences to AVOID

| Type | ❌ Example | Why It Fails |
|------|-----------|--------------|
| **Filler sentences** | "It should be noted that..." | No information added |
| **Tautologies** | "The results are what they are." | Circular, no meaning |
| **Obvious statements** | "Water is essential for life." | Adds no value to scientific context |
| **Vague assertions** | "This is very important." | No specific information |
| **Redundant restatements** | "In other words, the results show that X leads to Y." | Already said this |
| **Empty hedging** | "It might be possible that perhaps..." | No commitment, no information |

### 1.1 One Idea Per Sentence

**Express ONE idea per sentence.** This is non-negotiable.

#### Why This Matters

- Readers process one concept at a time
- Multiple ideas create confusion about what's important
- Complex sentences should still have ONE core message

#### ❌ Violations vs ✅ Corrections

| ❌ Multiple Ideas (Bad) | ✅ One Idea Per Sentence (Good) |
|------------------------|-------------------------------|
| The temperature increased by 5°C over the course of the experiment, which lasted three hours, and we observed a corresponding change in reaction rate. | **Sentence 1:** The temperature increased by 5°C during the three-hour experiment. **Sentence 2:** We observed a corresponding change in reaction rate. |
| Smith et al. (2020) demonstrated that protein X binds to Y, but later studies by Jones (2021) contradicted these findings, showing instead that Z is the primary binding partner. | **Sentence 1:** Smith et al. (2020) demonstrated that protein X binds to Y. **Sentence 2:** However, Jones (2021) showed that Z is the primary binding partner. |

#### When to Combine Ideas

Only combine ideas in one sentence when:
- They are **causally linked** AND
- The relationship IS the main point

> ✅ "Increasing temperature accelerated the reaction rate." (Cause-effect relationship IS the point)
>
> ❌ "Increasing temperature accelerated the reaction rate, and we used three different catalysts." (Unrelated ideas)

---

### 1.2 Topic Focus

**The grammatical subject of your sentence MUST be what you are writing about.**

#### The Topic-Subject Rule

| If your topic is... | Then the grammatical subject should be... |
|--------------------|------------------------------------------|
| An enzyme | The enzyme (not "the reaction") |
| A method | The method (not "the results") |
| A phenomenon | The phenomenon (not "we observed") |

#### ❌ Weak vs ✅ Strong Examples

| ❌ Wrong Subject (Weak) | ✅ Correct Subject (Strong) | Why |
|------------------------|---------------------------|-----|
| The reaction was catalyzed by the enzyme. | The enzyme catalyzed the reaction. | Topic is the enzyme |
| We observed an increase in temperature. | The temperature increased. | Topic is temperature |
| It was found that the protein binds to DNA. | The protein binds to DNA. | Topic is the protein |
| There was a significant difference between groups. | Group A differed significantly from Group B. | Topic is the groups |

#### How to Identify Your Topic

Ask yourself: "What am I trying to tell the reader about?"

- If discussing a **process** → The process is the subject
- If discussing a **result** → The result is the subject
- If discussing a **method** → The method is the subject

---

### 1.3 Complex Sentence Handling

#### The Main Clause Rule

**In complex sentences, the main idea MUST be in the main clause, NOT in a subordinate clause.**

#### Why This Matters

Readers naturally emphasize the main clause. If your key point is buried in a subordinate clause, it loses impact.

#### ❌ Main Idea in Subordinate Clause vs ✅ Main Idea in Main Clause

| ❌ Main Idea Buried | ✅ Main Idea in Main Clause |
|--------------------|---------------------------|
| Although the temperature increased significantly, which was unexpected given previous studies, the reaction rate remained constant. | Although the temperature increased significantly, the reaction rate remained constant. This stability was unexpected given previous studies. |
| Because we observed a correlation between X and Y, which has not been reported before, we conducted additional experiments. | We observed a novel correlation between X and Y. Consequently, we conducted additional experiments. |

#### The Phenomenon vs Observation Rule

**Focus on the phenomenon itself, NOT on the fact that you observed it.**

| ❌ Focus on Observation | ✅ Focus on Phenomenon |
|------------------------|----------------------|
| We observed that the protein folds rapidly. | The protein folds rapidly. |
| It was found that temperature affects the rate. | Temperature affects the rate. |
| Our data show that X inhibits Y. | X inhibits Y. |

**Exception**: When the act of observation IS relevant (e.g., in methods sections)

### 1.4 Cognitive Load Management

**Do NOT oblige readers to remember long pieces of text before knowing what to do with them.**

#### The Proximity Principle

**Keep together what goes together.**

| Element | Rule |
|---------|------|
| Subject and verb | Keep adjacent |
| Verb and object | Keep adjacent |
| Noun and its modifier | Keep adjacent |
| A claim and its evidence | Keep in same or adjacent sentence |

#### ❌ High Cognitive Load vs ✅ Low Cognitive Load

| ❌ High Load (Bad) | ✅ Low Load (Good) |
|-------------------|-------------------|
| The catalyst, which was prepared using a modified sol-gel method involving titanium isopropoxide as the precursor and calcined at 450°C for four hours under nitrogen atmosphere, increased the conversion rate. | The catalyst increased the conversion rate. It was prepared using a modified sol-gel method with titanium isopropoxide as the precursor and calcined at 450°C for four hours under nitrogen. |
| We found, after conducting extensive statistical analyses including ANOVA and post-hoc tests, that the difference was significant. | We found the difference was significant (ANOVA, p < 0.05). |

---

### 1.5 Conciseness Requirements

**Every word must earn its place.** If a word can be removed without losing clarity or accuracy, remove it.

#### The Deletion Test

For each word, ask: "If I delete this, does the meaning change?"

- **No** → Delete the word
- **Yes** → Keep the word

#### ❌ Wordy vs ✅ Concise

| ❌ Wordy | ✅ Concise | Words Saved |
|---------|----------|-------------|
| In order to | To | 2 |
| Due to the fact that | Because | 4 |
| A large number of | Many | 3 |
| In the event that | If | 3 |
| At this point in time | Now | 4 |
| It is important to note that | (Delete entirely) | 6 |
| The results of the study showed that | The study showed | 4 |

#### The Phrase Replacement Test

**Always try to replace long phrases with shorter ones.**

| Long Phrase | Shorter Alternative |
|------------|-------------------|
| Make an examination of | examine |
| Present a comparison of | compare |
| Be in agreement | agree |
| Perform an analysis of | analyze |
| Produce an improvement in | improve |
| Give a description of | describe |
| Conduct an investigation of | investigate |
| Make a decision | decide |

---

### 1.6 Sentence Length Guidelines

#### Optimal Sentence Length

- **Target**: 15-25 words per sentence (scientific writing)
- **Maximum**: 35 words (exceptional cases only)
- **Minimum**: No strict minimum, but avoid fragments

#### Length Warning Signs

| If your sentence has... | Action |
|------------------------|--------|
| More than 3 commas | Consider splitting |
| More than 2 clauses | Consider splitting |
| More than 35 words | Split into 2+ sentences |
| Multiple "and"/"or" | Consider splitting |

### ❌ Weak Examples vs ✅ Strong Examples

| ❌ Weak Example | ✅ Strong Example |
|----------------|------------------|
| Make an examination of... | examine |
| Present a comparison of... | compare |
| Be in agreement... | agree |
| Perform an analysis of... | analyze |
| Produce an improvement in... | improve |
| The catalyst produced a significant increase in conversion rate | The catalyst increased the conversion rate significantly |

## II. Verb Usage

Verbs are a major source of strength in a clause. Choose an appropriate verb and use it well.

### 2.1 Verb Tense Rules

#### Past Tense

Use to report what happened in the past:

- **Work done**
  - We collected blood samples from...
  - Groves et al. determined the growth rate of...

- **Work reported**
  - Jankowsky reported a similar growth rate...
  - In 2009, Chu published an alternative method to...

- **Observations**
  - The mice in Group A developed, on average, twice as much...
  - The conversion rate was close to 95%...

#### Present Tense

Use to express general truths:

- **General truths**
  - Microbes in the human gut have a profound influence on...
  - The Reynolds number provides a measure of...

- **Atemporal facts**
  - This paper presents the results of...
  - Section 3.1 explains the difference between...

#### Future Tense

Use for perspectives:

- **Perspectives**
  - In a follow-up experiment, we will study the role of...
  - The influence of temperature will be the object of future research...

#### Mixed Tenses

In complex sentences, you may need to combine different tenses:

> In 1905, Albert Einstein **postulated** (past tense) that the speed of light **is** (present tense) constant...

### 2.2 Active vs Passive Voice

#### Selection Principle

**Choose based on topic**: Place what you are discussing in the subject position

- If discussing the preprocessor → "The preprocessor sorts the two arrays"
- If discussing the arrays → "The two arrays are sorted by the preprocessor"

#### Problems with Passive Voice

1. **Poor readability**: Passive voice sentences are often less interesting or harder to read
2. **Overuse**: The desire for objectivity in scientific writing has led to passive voice overuse

#### Transformation Examples

| ❌ Passive Voice | ✅ Active Voice |
|-----------------|----------------|
| The temperature was measured... | The measured temperature of 253°C **suggests** a secondary reaction... |
| In this section, a discussion of... is presented | This section **discusses**... |

#### Clarify the Agent

When readers need to know "who", use active voice:

| ❌ Vague | ✅ Clear |
|---------|---------|
| The temperature is believed to be the cause... | Biologists believe the temperature to be... |
| | Keustermans et al. (1997) believe the temperature to be... |
| | We believe the temperature to be... |

### 2.3 Avoid Dangling Verb Forms

#### Dangling Verb Problem

Non-finite verb forms (infinitives `to do` or participles `doing`) need a subject.

#### ❌ Incorrect Examples

> To dissect its brain, the affected fly was mounted on a...
> (Implies the fly dissected its own brain)

> After aging for 72 hours at 50°C, we observed a shift in...
> (Implies the authors needed to age for 72 hours at 50°C)

#### ✅ Correction Methods

**Method 1: Change the subject**

> To dissect its brain, **we** mounted the affected fly on a...
> After aging for 72 hours at 50°C, **the samples** exhibited a shift in...

**Method 2: Change or remove the infinitive/participle**

> To have its brain dissected, the affected fly was mounted on a...
> After the samples aged for 72 hours at 50°C, we observed a shift in...

## III. Writing Mechanics

### 3.1 Abbreviation Usage

#### Basic Rules

- ❌ **Avoid overusing abbreviations**, especially acronyms
- ✅ Write all acronyms in **ALL UPPERCASE** (GNP, not gnp)

#### Introducing Abbreviations

Introduce systematically on first use:
1. Write the full expression first
2. Provide the acronym in parentheses
3. In the full expression, capitalize the letters that form the acronym

**Example**:
> we prepared **G**old **N**ano**P**articles (GNP) by...

#### Special Cases

- If the acronym is better known than the full expression, consider placing the acronym first:
  > The FALCON (Fission-Activated Laser Concept) program at...

- Common acronyms (like DNA) may not need introduction

- In abstracts, treat the abstract as a standalone document and introduce acronyms **separately**

### 3.2 Writing Numbers

#### Basic Rules

- Single-digit numbers (0-9): **Write as words** (three hours)
- Multi-digit numbers (10 and above): **Use numerals** (24 hours)

#### Exceptions Using Numerals (0-9)

- With abbreviated units: `3 mV`
- In dates and times: `3 October`, `3 pm`
- To identify figures: `Figure 3`
- For consistency when mixed with larger numbers: `series of 3, 7, and 24 experiments`

#### Exceptions Using Words (10+)

- At the beginning of a sentence or heading:
  > **T**wo thousand eight was a challenging year for...
  >
  > **T**he year 2008 was challenging for...

### 3.3 Capitalization

#### When to Use Capitals

- **Beginnings**: Start of sentences, headings, etc.
- **Proper nouns**: Including nouns describing groups (compare "physics" and "the Physics Department")
- **Numbered items**: Compare "in the next figure" and "in Figure 2"
- **Specific words**: Days of the week, months, nationality adjectives, etc.

#### ❌ When NOT to Use Capitals

Do not capitalize common nouns. **Resist** the temptation to glorify concepts, techniques, or compounds:

- ✅ finite-element method (❌ Finite-Element Method)
- ✅ mass spectrometry (❌ Mass Spectrometry)
- ✅ carbon dioxide (❌ Carbon Dioxide)

### 3.4 Hyphen Usage

#### Basic Principle

Use hyphens to **clarify relationships in chains of words**.

#### Examples

- `low temperature impact` → suggests a low impact of temperature
- `low-temperature impact` → suggests impact of or at low temperature

#### No Hyphen Needed

- **Adverbs**: `a highly interesting paper` (no hyphen needed)
  - Because "highly" can only qualify "interesting", not "paper"

#### Prefix Rules

**General rule**: Do not use a hyphen with a prefix
- multichannel, nonlinear, preamplifier, postdoctoral, realign

**Exceptions**:
- Separate vowels that would otherwise be read together: `pre-embryo`
- When the original word starts with a capital: `pre-Columbian`

### 3.5 Punctuation

#### Comma Rules

**Rule 1**: Insert a comma between the subject of the main clause and whatever comes in front of it
> Surprisingly, the temperature did not increase.
> (Even if short, this comma helps and never hurts the meaning)

**Rule 2**: In series of three or more items, separate with commas
> red, white, and blue
> yesterday, today, or tomorrow

**Rule 3**: Do NOT use a comma for a series of two items
> black and white

#### List Punctuation

Use the same punctuation in displayed lists as in normal text:

> The system is fast, flexible, and reliable.

> The system is
> - fast,
> - flexible,
> - reliable.

## IV. Comprehensive Checklist

### 0. Purpose & Meaning (CRITICAL - Check First)
- [ ] **Does every sentence have a clear purpose?** (Delete sentences that don't)
- [ ] **Does every sentence convey actual information?** (No filler, no tautologies)
- [ ] **Is every claim supported?** (No unsupported assertions)
- [ ] **Is there any sentence that could be deleted without losing meaning?** (If yes, delete it)
- [ ] **Are there any "It is important to note that..." or similar filler phrases?** (Delete them)

### 1. Sentence Level
- [ ] **One idea per sentence?** (Split sentences with multiple ideas)
- [ ] **Is the topic the grammatical subject?** (Check subject-verb alignment)
- [ ] **In complex sentences, is the main idea in the main clause?** (Not buried in subordinate clause)
- [ ] **Is related content kept together?** (Subject-verb, verb-object proximity)
- [ ] **Is sentence length appropriate?** (15-25 words ideal, max 35)
- [ ] **Does the sentence focus on the phenomenon, not the observation?** (Unless methods section)

### 2. Word Economy
- [ ] **Can any word be deleted without losing meaning?** (Delete it)
- [ ] **Are there long phrases that could be shorter?** (Replace them)
- [ ] **Are there any redundant expressions?** (e.g., "in order to" → "to")
- [ ] **Is every word earning its place?** (Apply deletion test)

### 3. Verb Level
- [ ] **Are strong verbs used instead of weak verb + noun?** (e.g., "analyze" not "perform analysis of")
- [ ] **Is the tense correct?**
  - [ ] Past tense for: work done, work reported, observations
  - [ ] Present tense for: general truths, paper content
  - [ ] Future tense for: perspectives only
- [ ] **Is active/passive voice chosen based on topic?** (Subject = what you're discussing)
- [ ] **Are dangling verb forms avoided?** (Check infinitives and participles)

### 4. Clarity & Logic
- [ ] **Can a non-expert understand the main point?** (Test with "tell a friend" rule)
- [ ] **Is the logical flow clear?** (Each sentence follows from previous)
- [ ] **Are transitions meaningful?** (Not just "Moving on...")
- [ ] **Is there any ambiguity?** (Clarify agent when needed)

### 5. Mechanics Level
- [ ] **Are abbreviations properly introduced?** (Full term first, then acronym in parentheses)
- [ ] **Are abbreviations necessary?** (Only use if appearing multiple times)
- [ ] **Does number writing follow conventions?**
  - [ ] 0-9 as words (except with units, dates, figures)
  - [ ] 10+ as numerals (except sentence start)
- [ ] **Is capitalization appropriate?** (Not overused on common nouns)
- [ ] **Are hyphens correctly used?** (To clarify word relationships, not with prefixes generally)
- [ ] **Is punctuation correct?**
  - [ ] Comma after introductory elements
  - [ ] Serial commas for 3+ items
  - [ ] No comma for 2 items

### 6. Final Review
- [ ] **Read aloud: Does it flow naturally?**
- [ ] **Would you say this to a friend explaining your work?**
- [ ] **Is the text as short as it can be while remaining clear and accurate?**

## IV-B. Sentence-Level Review Protocol

For each sentence, apply this systematic check:

```
FOR EACH SENTENCE:
1. PURPOSE CHECK: What is this sentence doing?
   → If no clear purpose: DELETE

2. INFORMATION CHECK: What new information does this provide?
   → If none: DELETE or MERGE with another sentence

3. FOCUS CHECK: Is the grammatical subject = the topic?
   → If not: REWRITE with correct subject

4. LENGTH CHECK: Count words
   → If >35: SPLIT into multiple sentences

5. IDEA CHECK: How many distinct ideas?
   → If >1: SPLIT into separate sentences

6. CLAUSE CHECK: Is the main point in the main clause?
   → If not: RESTRUCTURE

7. CONCISENESS CHECK: Can any word be deleted?
   → If yes: DELETE it

8. VERB CHECK: Is the verb strong and appropriate?
   → If weak verb + noun: REPLACE with strong verb

PASS ALL CHECKS → Sentence is acceptable
FAIL ANY CHECK → Revise sentence
```

## V. Quick Reference

### Tense Selection

| Content Type | Tense |
|-------------|-------|
| Work done | Past tense |
| Work reported | Past tense |
| Observations | Past tense |
| General truths | Present tense |
| Paper content | Present tense |
| Future plans | Future tense |

### Voice Selection

| Situation | Recommendation |
|-----------|----------------|
| Emphasize the agent | Active voice |
| Emphasize the object | Passive voice |
| Need to clarify "who" | Active voice |
| Improve readability | Prefer active voice |

### Number Format

| Range | Format |
|-------|--------|
| 0-9 | Words (three) |
| 10+ | Numerals (24) |
| With units | Numerals (3 mV) |
| Sentence start | Words (Twenty) |
